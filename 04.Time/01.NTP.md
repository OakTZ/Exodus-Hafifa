
### Goals
- The trainee will understand why accurate time matters
- The trainee will understand how NTP works, including strata and how a client picks its source
- The trainee will know what **chrony** (`chronyd`) does, and how it is configured as a client and as a time server
- The trainee will know why **ntpd** is considered legacy, and what replaced it

### Why chrony and not ntpd
`ntpd` is the classic NTP daemon. Red Hat removed it from RHEL 8 and later, and chrony is the default there. Debian and Ubuntu also use chrony in many releases. Chrony syncs faster, copes better with unstable networks and virtual machines, and needs fewer resources. In this module you use chrony only.

- **Theory** - read about NTP and answer:
  - What is a **stratum**? What is the stratum of a reference clock (e.g. GPS or an atomic clock), and what is the stratum of a client that syncs from it?
  - Explain the NTP exchange: the four timestamps, and how a client calculates **offset** and **round-trip delay** from them
  - How does a client choose between several sources? Why does NTP use more than one source?
  - Why must time be accurate for TLS certificates, Kerberos, logs, and distributed systems such as etcd leases?
  - What is the difference between **slewing** and **stepping** a clock? When does chrony step, and why is stepping risky?
  - Why does NTP use **UDP port 123**? Which firewall rule would allow a client to reach your time server?

  - Which NTP source do Compute Engine VMs use by default? What is its IP address?
  - Why is it a good idea to use it instead of a public pool, and what does "leap smearing" mean?
